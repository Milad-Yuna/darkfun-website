01۰۱

The Name نام

{{USE:wm-fa:wm:جستجو}} jos · to · JOO  /dʒos.toˈdʒuː/  noun. The search; the quest; the act of seeking.
جستJOST · SOUGHT
+
وO · AND
+
جوJU · SEEK

Jostoju is an everyday Persian word. You use it when you look for your keys, and a poet uses it when looking for God.

It is built like گفت‌وگو (conversation): the past stem of the verb jostan, to seek, then o, "and", then the present stem. Sought-and-seek. The word holds what has already been found and what is still being looked for. That is exactly the work of a film studio.

Spelling rule: the brand name is always written joined, جستجو, in the logo and in running text. The Academy spelling جست‌وجو is correct Persian, but a brand name has one form. In Latin script it is always Jostoju, lowercase in the logo.

Our verb is in the verse. جسته‌ایم, "we have searched", is the same verb as our name. This couplet is the source of our master tagline.

Attar's quest. In The Conference of the Birds (1177), the birds of the world set out to find the Simorgh, their king. They cross seven valleys, and the first is Talab, the quest. Thirty birds arrive. They find that the Simorgh is si morgh, "thirty birds": themselves. A search that ends in a mirror is a good description of what a film does. The seven valleys also give us our production process (chapter 15).

02۰۲

Origin ریشه

Our origin story. It goes on the About page, in pitch decks and in every interview.

Long before the pixel, Persia had the knot.

The knot گره

A Persian carpet is woven knot by knot from a naqsheh, a design painted on squared paper where one square is one knot. Iranian weavers still measure fineness in raj: knots per 7 cm.

The dot نقطه

Persian calligraphers measure every letter in rhombic dots made with the reed pen. An alef is a set number of dots tall. The dot is a unit of proportion, as a pixel is.

The pigment رنگ

Miniature painters ground lapis lazuli from Badakhshan for their skies and laid gold leaf for their suns. Every colour came from a real material with a real source.

The screen پرده

In coffee houses and town squares, storytellers unrolled a painted canvas, the pardeh, and performed the Shahnameh in front of it. In Persian, the cinema screen is still called a pardeh.

> 4K

The Ardabil Carpet (1539–40) holds tens of millions of knots, more than a 4K film frame holds pixels (8.3 million). It is also signed: "the work of a servant of the court, Maqsud of Kashan."

Jostoju picks up that thread with today's loom. AI image and video models are the strongest picture-making tools since the camera, and most of what they produce looks like it came from nowhere. We point them somewhere: at a thousand years of Persian image-making, and at stories that have waited a long time for a budget.

We work the way the royal ketabkhaneh workshops did. There is a design before the first knot, a master on every row and a name on the finished work. We make commissioned films for brands and artists, and original films of our own, in Persian and English.

05۰۵

Positioning جایگاه

The market

AI film studios are multiplying, and almost all of them sell the same thing: speed, scale and a Hollywood look. Most AI video, from agencies and freelancers alike, shares one glossy, placeless style. Traditional production houses own cultural depth and trust, but they are slow, expensive and bound to what a camera can reach.

Nobody owns a studio that is both rooted in a specific visual culture and authored by directors who use AI as a tool. That gap is Jostoju's.

For brands, artists and audiences who want Persian stories told with cinematic ambition, Jostoju is the AI film studio that weaves a thousand years of Persian image-making into new films, directed by human hands and written natively in Persian and English.

Voice

Jostoju speaks with a storyteller's warmth and a craftsman's precision: curious, rhythmic, plain and honest.

Curious کنجکاو

We ask before we answer. Our first line is often a question, and we show our research instead of announcing that we did it.

Rhythmic آهنگین

Persian prose loves cadence, and so do we: short lines and parallel phrases. Poetic sometimes, purple never.

Precise دقیق

We name the real thing: madder red, the 1522 Shahnameh, a 24-frame shot. Never "vibrant ancient magic".

Warm گرم

Persian hospitality in writing. We say "we" and "you", we thank people properly, and we never talk down.

Honest about the machine صادق

We say what was made with AI and what was made by hand. We never overclaim and never hide the tools.

Quietly confident آرام و مطمئن

No hype words. The work does the shouting. One strong claim beats three adjectives.

Bilingual by birth دوزبانه

Persian is never a translation of English, or the other way round. Each language is written first in its own voice.

Writing in two languages
  1. Write each language natively. Draft the Persian in Persian, then align the meaning with the English. Never machine-translate brand copy.
  2. On Persian-facing surfaces, Persian comes first. On global surfaces, English comes first. When both share a line, Persian sits on the right and English on the left, as in the logo.
  3. Use Persian characters ی and ک, never the Arabic forms ي and ك.
  4. Use the half-space (ZWNJ) properly: می‌سازیم, فیلم‌ها, قصه‌ها.
  5. Use Persian digits (۰۱۲۳۴۵۶۷۸۹) in Persian text. Keep Latin digits for timecodes, hex codes and specs.
  6. Never letter-space Persian, never fake an italic and never stretch it with kashida to justify body text.
  7. Romanise simply, without diacritics in the UI: Jostoju, Kargah, Pardeh, Naqsheh, Lajvard.

The symbol · The Missing Knot · گره گمشده

Twenty-four diamond knots sit on a 5 × 5 diagonal lattice, the same grid a weaver reads from a naqsheh. Each knot is also the rhombic dot of the calligrapher's pen. The centre knot is missing on purpose.

There is a weavers' legend that every Persian carpet holds one deliberate flaw, because only God is perfect. Our gap means something else: the thing we are still looking for. It is the story, the shot, "what cannot be found". In motion, a frame of film appears in that space for a moment.

16Outer ring. The hashiyeh, or border: tradition and craft. Set in ink, or wool on dark grounds.
8Inner ring. The toranj, or medallion: the story. Set in madder, or saffron on dark grounds.
0The missing knot: the quest. It is never filled with flat colour.
aThe lattice step. Every logo measurement is a multiple of a.

The wordmarks come from Instrument Serif (Latin) and Markazi Text Bold (Persian), converted to outlines. In both, the dots of the j and of the ج are replaced by knots. Each script has two of them, in matching positions.

Lockups

{{USE:lk-h:-:Horizontal lockup with clear space}} 2a2a

Keep at least 2a clear on every side, where a is the lattice step. That is the width of one knot plus its gap.

Pattern نقش

Every Jostoju pattern is drawn as a naqsheh: one square is one knot. Patterns are built on the same grid as the logo, so the whole system is made of a single unit.

{{NAQSHEH}}

The hashiyeh border, drawn on graph paper. Every pattern in the system is stored like this in brand/source/gen_patterns.py, so a designer, a weaver or a developer can read it.

Hashiyeh حاشیه

Border bands. They frame pages, open sections and run along film slates. Use only the top and bottom edges.

Matn متن

The field. A quiet all-over lattice at 4–8% opacity, behind large logos and on empty states. Never behind body text.

Lachak لچک

Corner pieces. They frame a quote, a poster or a key card, as the carpet frames its field. Use them on one element per view.

Toranj ترنج

The medallion: the symbol grown outward, ring by ring. For covers, posters and loading screens. Its centre always stays open.

Zar-afshan زرافشان

Gold sprinkle, from the gold-flecked margins of manuscripts. For covers and celebrations, such as Nowruz and premieres.

Abrash ابرش

The gentle colour banding in a hand-dyed carpet. Large colour fields may carry it, with subtle horizontal variation and grain, so they never look plastic.
Signature move · break the frame

Persian miniature painters ruled a frame around each picture, then let a tree, a horse or a cliff grow past it into the margin. We do the same: one element per layout crosses its frame.

On the web, this is an image or illustration that overlaps its border or the next section. In film, it is a title or character that crosses the letterbox. Use it once per view; used twice, it stops being special.

13۱۳

Imagery and Film Language زبان تصویر

Miniature logic, cinema light. These rules guide every prompt, every frame and every grade.

01

Build in planes

Stack space vertically, as a miniature does: foreground at the bottom, distance at the top, a high horizon and readable silhouettes. Ask for "flat layered depth, elevated viewpoint" before "shallow depth of field".

02

More than one moment

Let a frame hold a story: two beats of the same journey, a split screen, a scene inside a scene. Miniatures did this for centuries.

03

Colour as meaning

Skies can be gold or lapis. Pick palettes from the eight dyes and use them for what they mean, not for realism.

04

Light through orosi

Coloured, patterned light from stained-glass sash windows, lanterns and low sun through lattice. Every light has a source; no sourceless AI glow.

05

Real, specific faces

Cast the many peoples of Iran: Persian, Azeri, Kurdish, Lur, Baluch, Turkmen, Gilak, Arab and Armenian Iranians. Age, character and imperfection over generic AI beauty.

06

Keep the grain

Wool, plaster, brass, skin pores and film grain. Texture is proof of a hand. Waxy, glossy, over-sharpened surfaces are off-brand.

Grade · the Jostoju look
Shadows · Nil
Low mids · Lajvard
Reds held deep
Skin protected
Highlights · Za'faran

Split-tone: indigo-blue shadows and warm saffron highlights. Keep madder reds deep; never let them drift orange. Protect skin tones. Keep blacks slightly lifted, like ink on paper, not crushed.

AI quality gate · before anything ships
  • No AI-written Persian. Models garble the script. Replace every sign, book and inscription with real type in post.
  • Region-correct design. Isfahan is not Marrakech. Check arches, tiles and costume against the period: Safavid, Qajar and Pahlavi are different.
  • Continuity. Ornament, costume pattern, hands and eyes must hold from shot to shot.
  • Consent. No real person's face or voice without written consent.
Never
  • Flying carpets, genies, belly dancers or "Arabian Nights" mash-ups
  • Sepia or yellow "Middle East" filters
  • News and war imagery used as shorthand for Iran
  • The outsider's gaze: Iranians only as victims, villains or exotica
  • Generic AI gloss: waxy skin, perfect symmetry, glow everywhere
  • Fake Persian script, or Persian text set left-to-right
14۱۴

Motion and Sound حرکت و صدا

Carpets are woven from the bottom row up, so Jostoju moves the same way: row by row, one knot at a time.

{{WEAVE}}
01

Woven, bottom to top

Reveals rise row by row, the way a carpet grows on the loom. Never fly elements in from the sides.

02

One knot at a time

Stagger children 40–110 ms apart. Everything appearing at once is a cut, not a reveal.

03

The shuttle ease

A quick start and a long, soft landing, like a shuttle thrown across the warp: cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1).

04

Cut like a naqqal

Hold on images longer than the feed expects. Cut on the story's beat. No whooshes, lens flares or speed ramps for their own sake.

05

The knot dissolve

Our signature transition: frame A dissolves into frame B through the knot grid, one diamond cell at a time.

The Seven Valleys هفت وادی

Attar's birds crossed seven valleys to find the Simorgh. Every Jostoju film crosses the same seven, in order. The process is the product, and it is what we show clients.

  1. 1طلبQuestTalab · Discovery

    We turn the brief into a question. Who is this for, and what should they feel? We listen first, and research the audience and the references.

    Out: the creative question, one page
  2. 2عشقLoveEshgh · Concept

    We look for the idea we can't stop thinking about. If we aren't in love with it, the audience won't be.

    Out: treatment and three key frames
  3. 3معرفتKnowingMa'refat · World-building

    Research, look book, characters, locations, style frames, script and storyboard. We design the world before we weave it.

    Out: world bible and animatic
  4. 4استغناDetachmentEsteghna · Generation

    We generate hundreds of takes and keep the few that are true. Being detached from what the machine made is the job.

    Out: selects, approved shot by shot
  5. 5توحیدUnityTowhid · Finishing

    Edit, sound, music, grade and Persian type. Hundreds of fragments become one film.

    Out: picture lock and final mix
  6. 6حیرتWonderHeyrat · Delivery

    Masters for every screen and both languages: cut-downs, verticals, subtitles and stills. Then comes the premiere.

    Out: masters and release kit
  7. 7فقر و فناLetting goFaqr-o-Fana · Release

    The film now belongs to its audience. We write down what we learned, publish the case study and start the next search.

    Out: case study and lessons learned
