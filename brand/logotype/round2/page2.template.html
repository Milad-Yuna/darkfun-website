The quest, made visible

Built on the two typefaces you chose: Amiri, the classical Naskh you liked, and Noto Sans Arabic Black, the free face closest to the Fontiran specimen. Both are open-licensed, so we can change them and use them in the logo. In every version the dots are the brand's diamond knots and the vowel marks are the second colour.

The idea

Sought, and still seeking

جستجو is two halves of one verb: jost, sought (past), and ju, seek (present). So the dot of the first ج, in the "sought" half, is a solid knot: found. The dot of the last ج, in the "seek" half, is an empty knot: still being looked for.

It's Rumi's «آنکه یافت می‌نشود، آنم آرزوست», what cannot be found is what I long for, and the same missing knot as the brand symbol.

The four quest moves · Amiri

Each idea on its own

{{USE:amiri-missing::Missing knot version}}

The Missing Knot گره گمشده

The dot of "seek" is empty. It's the quietest move and ties the logotype to the symbol.

{{USE:amiri-road::Road version}}

The Road راه

A long keshideh through the سـ: the quest as distance travelled. This is the classical calligrapher's gesture.

{{USE:amiri-thread::Thread version}}

The Thread سرنخ

The و tail runs on as a thread and ends in a knot. In Persian, sar-e nakh, the end of the thread, means a clue.

{{USE:amiri-ascent::Ascent version}}

The Ascent عروج

The vowels rise from right to left like Attar's birds flying toward the Simorgh. Each one still sits over its own letter.

{{USE:amiri-path::Path version}}

Road and thread راه و سرنخ

The road, then the thread, ending in the empty knot. The richest version, best kept for large formats.
